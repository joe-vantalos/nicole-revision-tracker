# Multiple-choice rewrite: questions to review

The wrong options of 599 single best answer questions were rewritten so the right answer can no longer be spotted by its length or wording (see `node tools/audit-mcq.js`). Every rewrite was checked by a second, independent reviewer for veterinary accuracy. 30 errors found in that check were corrected. The questions below are the ones where the reviewer was not fully sure; the rewrite was kept, and a person who knows the subject should confirm or adjust them.

The right answer is marked **(answer)**.

## E07-R1-06

During milking, teat stimulation triggers oxytocin release and milk let-down in the dairy cow. On which cells does oxytocin act to cause milk ejection?

- A. Secretory alveolar epithelial cells, increasing milk synthesis
- B. Smooth muscle cells of the teat sphincter, relaxing it
- C. Endothelial cells of mammary capillaries, causing vasodilation
- D. Smooth muscle in the wall of the gland and teat cisterns
- E. Myoepithelial cells surrounding the alveoli and small ducts **(answer)**

**Why it is listed:** Removing 'only' turned 'smooth muscle of the gland cistern only' into 'smooth muscle in the wall of the gland and teat cisterns'; not the best answer and not an established oxytocin target, but now a softer, partly plausible statement.

## E34-R1-06

A hypothyroid dog has an area of hair on the flank that has failed to regrow for four months after clipping. In which phase of the hair cycle are most of the follicles in this area likely to be arrested?

- A. Catagen
- B. Anagen
- C. Telogen **(answer)**
- D. Proanagen
- E. Late catagen

**Why it is listed:** 'Proanagen' is a real but obscure subdivision of anagen (proanagen/metanagen), so it is not invented, but a student may still read it as made up. Restoring the original 'Exogen' fails the length-rank rule (Telogen moves to rank 3), so I left it. 'Late catagen' is real and clearly wrong.

## G35-R1-02

Which type of hypersensitivity can be transferred to a naive animal by T lymphocytes from a sensitised animal, but not by its serum?

- A. Type II
- B. Type IV **(answer)**
- C. Type I (late)
- D. Type I
- E. Type III

**Why it is listed:** 'Type I (late)' is a terse way of writing the late-phase type I reaction. It is accurate and correctly wrong, because the reaction is IgE-initiated and can be transferred with serum, but it reads awkwardly. Fuller wordings such as 'Type I late-phase' fail the length ratio against the 7-character key.

## E17-R2-03

A 5-year-old female bearded collie has chronic intermittent vomiting and weight loss. Sodium and potassium are normal; she has low albumin, low cholesterol and no stress leukogram. Basal cortisol is 8 nmol/L. What is the most appropriate next step?

- A. Low-dose dexamethasone suppression test to confirm hyperadrenocorticism
- B. Repeat the sodium:potassium ratio in 6 months before any further testing
- C. Start fludrocortisone treatment immediately without further testing
- D. Rule out hypoadrenocorticism, because sodium and potassium are normal
- E. ACTH stimulation test to confirm atypical hypoadrenocorticism **(answer)**

**Why it is listed:** The key now says 'atypical hypoadrenocorticism' instead of 'glucocorticoid-deficient hypoadrenocorticism'. Both terms describe this eunatraemic, eukalaemic dog, and the explanation defines the term, so I left it. Some current texts prefer 'glucocorticoid-deficient' because some 'atypical' dogs also lack aldosterone. Restoring the original term would break the length rank.
